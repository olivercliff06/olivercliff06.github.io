VentureXR Malaysia

An inclusive, multilingual XR companion for heritage, food and walking

Assignment response: three proposed solutions, technical architecture and high fidelity alpha prototype

Executive summary

VentureXR is a hybrid mobile and web concept that layers carefully sourced digital experiences over real places. It combines three connected functions: heritage reconstructions, multilingual menu interpretation and accessible walking routes. Malaysia is a strong pilot context: Tourism Malaysia counted 37.96 million visitor arrivals and RM106.8 billion in receipts in 2024, while DOSM reports tourism value added of RM323.0 billion in 2025. These figures support testing a place-based visitor product, but do not by themselves prove demand for XR. [[1], [2]] The concept also aligns with the National Tourism Policy’s smart, sustainable and inclusive tourism direction. [[3]]

The prototype is a responsive, clickable alpha with five navigable views: an overview plus three feature screens plus the My Trip support screen. Its interactions are front-end demonstrations; production camera tracking, live maps, translation, accounts and data services require the implementation described in Question 2.

Question 1 · Three proposed XR solutions

The three features form one app, designed first for Malaysian destinations and extensible to other cities and protected landscapes. George Town and Melaka provide a compelling initial heritage context because UNESCO describes their long-running, multicultural trading history and living tangible and intangible heritage. [[4]] The concept is a product proposal, not a claim that each feature has already been user-tested.

1. Adventure · place-based heritage across time

A camera-led AR experience reconstructs selected historic streets, buildings or objects in context, then lets visitors compare an archival scene with the present view. A site card supplies a short, sourced narrative, audio narration, captions, language choices and a clear indication of which elements are documented versus interpretive. At a designated site, the app first tries a location anchor; where visual positioning is unavailable or inaccurate, it falls back to a QR/image marker and a 2D story card. Users can save a destination pack before travel. This uses established geospatial anchors and image-triggered AR patterns rather than assuming that GPS alone can align a 3D scene. [[7], [10]] Heritage content is reviewed with local custodians and avoids presenting speculative reconstruction as fact. The goal is deeper interpretation and distributed visits, not a replacement for conservation signage.

2. Foodie · multilingual menu and ingredient companion

Visitors point their phone at a printed menu to obtain translated dish names, descriptions, ingredient notes and pronunciation. A preference panel lets users flag vegetarian choices, allergens to ask about, and requests such as halal information; the app never certifies a meal or treats an automated translation as a safety guarantee. Core OCR and casual translation can be processed on-device where supported, and the user can download language packs. Google documents on-device translation across 50+ languages, while cautioning that it is intended for casual translation and language pairs can vary in quality. [[12]] The prototype therefore displays a plain-language prompt to confirm ingredients with the vendor. The feature can also link to a participating vendor’s story and route, with an opt-in listing model.

3. Trail Companion · inclusive and context-aware walking

A route planner offers visitor-selectable priorities such as step-free segments, fewer kerbs, shade, shorter distance and avoiding busy roads. It displays the source and confidence of accessibility attributes, marks unknown data as unknown, and offers a text list and audio prompts in addition to the map. AR can show a simple directional cue at a verified junction, but the main route remains available without the camera and prompts users to stop before looking at the screen. Outdoor scene semantics can identify coarse scene classes such as road, sidewalk, building and tree for context, but these model outputs are not a substitute for verified accessibility survey data. [[11]] Geospatial location AR can support place-level guidance where coverage and device conditions permit. [[7]] The user can report a barrier, which is stored as unverified until checked.

Value, audience and feasibility

Primary audiences are international and domestic visitors, local residents, people with mobility or sensory access needs, families and destination publishers. Tourism Malaysia’s 2024 figures indicate a large visitor base; the 2025 DOSM account reports tourism’s substantial economic contribution. These are market context, not adoption forecasts. [[1], [2]] The National Tourism Policy explicitly includes smart tourism, sustainable and responsible tourism, and inclusion in its transformation strategies. [[3]]

  • Differentiation: the features connect heritage, food and mobility through one place catalogue and downloadable trip packs.
  • Business and partnership model: a destination organization funds the core platform; approved museums, councils, park managers and businesses publish content. Sponsored listings are labelled and cannot affect safety or accessibility rankings.
  • Feasible rollout: pilot one compact route in George Town, then add a second Malaysian destination. Build the responsive discovery and publishing surfaces first; deliver stable AR with Unity AR Foundation, which supports a shared Android/iOS app path with platform-specific plugins. [[6]]
  • Success measures: task completion for finding a heritage experience and starting a route; language-card usefulness; downloads and offline completion; reported route barriers resolved; editorial review and correction turnaround; and opt-in user satisfaction. Do not treat camera time or location traces as success metrics.

Question 2 · Feasible technical software architecture

The recommended product is a hybrid mobile app for camera-heavy experiences plus a responsive Progressive Web App (PWA) for discovery and content publishing. AR Foundation provides a shared Unity layer while ARCore and ARKit adapters expose platform capabilities. A separate web console lets approved partners create and review content without requiring a mobile release for each change. The system uses an API gateway, modular content and route services, spatial data storage, object delivery and explicit offline behaviour.

This architecture also supports the Malaysia Digital Economy Blueprint’s emphasis on stronger digital infrastructure, cybersecurity and accessible online services. [[5]]

Layer and component explanation

  • Mobile experience: Unity with AR Foundation handles camera rendering, anchors, image tracking, scene placement and platform abstraction. ARCore geospatial anchors use device sensors and VPS where coverage is present; ARKit geotracking combines location sensors and world tracking on supported Apple devices. These capabilities have different coverage, accuracy and hardware requirements, so the app checks availability at runtime and falls back to image markers, local anchors or non-AR cards. [[6], [7], [9], [10]]
  • Web surfaces: a responsive PWA provides search, trip planning, story playback, language selection and content tools. WebXR can be an optional browser capability, not the baseline: support is limited to specific browsers/devices and secure contexts, so native AR remains the dependable camera path. [[13]]
  • Application API: an HTTPS API validates user, publisher and anonymous visitor requests. OIDC authentication, role-based authorization, rate limits and audit logs separate public reading from partner authoring. Experience services expose catalogue search, route queries, user preferences, pack manifests and contribution workflows.
  • Data and media: PostgreSQL with PostGIS stores places, route geometry, accessibility attributes, content metadata, consent records and moderation states. Object storage retains versioned 3D models, textures, audio, captions and thumbnails behind a CDN. A search index supports multilingual titles and tags. Every content record should include author/source, rights, language, review date and confidence.
  • XR and language integrations: the AR service stores anchor identifiers and content metadata, not raw video. ARCore Geospatial API supports remote location-based placement in VPS-covered areas, and Cloud Anchors can share a local scene online. [[7], [8]] ARKit geotracking is an iOS-specific option. [[9]] Augmented image databases let the client identify curated printed markers locally. [[10]] On-device translation can reduce network use and avoid uploading scanned menu text, but users receive a clear quality caveat. [[12]]
  • Offline mode: before a trip, the user downloads an explicit destination pack containing app shell, selected stories, text, low-resolution image/3D assets, route summaries and language files. A service worker and local database serve cached content, while a sync queue submits feedback later. Service workers can cache resources and support offline-first behaviour. [[14]] Live geospatial localization, fresh closures, shared anchors and remote moderation remain online-only; the UI states pack freshness and unavailable functions.

Key flows

  • A. Discover and download: PWA requests catalogue and route metadata → API checks public scope → PostGIS-backed queries return nearby records → CDN serves previews → selected pack downloads and version is stored locally.
  • B. Start a heritage AR scene: client requests camera/location consent → checks ARCore/ARKit capability and permission → tries geospatial localization → if unavailable, asks the visitor to scan the site marker → retrieves the matching asset manifest → renders the anchor locally → records only minimal, consented diagnostics.
  • C. Translate a menu: camera text recognition runs locally → translation runs locally when the language model is available → uncertain words and allergen notes are labelled → the user sees the translated card and a vendor confirmation prompt. No menu image is uploaded by default.
  • D. Publish destination content: an authorized destination editor submits a heritage, menu or route update → the service checks media and required fields → an editor reviews sources, rights and accessibility → approved content is versioned and delivered through the CDN.

Privacy, safety and operational decisions

Use data minimization: ask for camera and precise location only when the chosen feature needs them; let the visitor use saved content without an account; retain location history only if the user explicitly saves a trip; and provide deletion and correction controls. Malaysia’s Personal Data Protection principles cover notice and choice, disclosure purpose, security, retention, data integrity and access. [[15]] The app should provide understandable notices, consent records, retention limits and vendor review, with legal review for the final deployment and any cross-border processing.

Design for WCAG 2.2 AA as the web content target: semantic headings and controls, keyboard operation, visible focus, scalable text, sufficient contrast, captions/transcripts, reduced motion and non-AR alternatives. [[16]] Do not depend on colour, camera gestures or spatial audio alone. The actual AR session must pause when the app is backgrounded and show a short environmental safety reminder.

Reliability decisions include graceful capability detection, an on-screen fallback when localization fails, content versioning, backups, rate limiting, CDN monitoring and a moderation audit. Accessibility and road-safety data are labelled with provenance and last-verified date. Automated visual classification never asserts a path is wheelchair accessible.

Question 3 · High fidelity alpha prototype and UX

Open the accompanying Open the interactive prototype file in a modern browser. It is a self-contained front-end demonstration with responsive desktop and mobile layouts, five navigable views, feature cards, route settings, selectable chips, scan/start buttons, story playback prompts, saved-state feedback and toast confirmations. It uses illustrative placeholders rather than live XR sensors or production map/heritage media.

UX rationale and alpha boundaries

  • Navigation: three primary feature destinations remain one tap away, with Overview and My Trip alongside them; the overview explains the product and offers clear launch points. Mobile uses a bottom navigation bar, while larger screens use a left rail.
  • Trust: confidence, provenance, consent, offline status and content limitations are visible near the feature that needs them. Medical/allergy and access claims are not automated promises.
  • Accessibility: large type hierarchy, clear labels, non-colour-only route cues, captions/transcript intent, direct buttons and text alternatives reduce reliance on XR interaction. WCAG 2.2 remains the implementation target rather than a claim of audited conformance. [[16]]
  • Alpha boundary: this prototype demonstrates navigation and interaction states only. It does not invoke camera APIs, ARCore/ARKit, GPS, actual OCR/translation, live routing, sign-in, storage or content publishing. A next build would validate these modules on supported devices and run usability sessions with visitors and destination editors.
